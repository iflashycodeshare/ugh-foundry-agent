**Consensus**

- Every option retains all Must BR/NFR/ARC/DISC obligations and all 39 scenarios; no producer claims that speed or cost permits a gate bypass.

- All nine preserve architecture neutrality and identify named ARC standards as constraints rather than choices.

**Divergence**

- A and B optimize through reusable capability/evidence increments; C organizes around control-obligation families and source authority distinctions.
- A2/B2/C2 differ on the fast path: A emphasizes parallel capability streams, B emphasizes an acceptance spine and thin end-to-end proof, and C explicitly places freshness interpretation and refusal-routing confirmation on the critical path.
- Comprehensive options differ in emphasis: A3 foregrounds tension proofs, B3 foregrounds broad operational and recovery assurance, and C3 foregrounds separately evidencable source obligations.

**Unique insights**

- **A:** Names the citation authorization side-channel and the audit-versus-diagnostic log-plane distinction explicitly.
- **B:** Separates a scenario-level acceptance spine from parallel evidence streams, providing a concrete guard against semantic drift.
- **C:** Preserves the distinction between contractual SOW authority, newly discovered DISC obligations, and ELAB refinement; treats elapsed gate time as the relevant speed constraint; and introduces sourced operational-baseline tracking without turning it into new product scope.

**Dominated options:** No option is strictly dominated. The least-cost, shortest-time, and comprehensive strategies intentionally optimize different objectives while preserving the same mandatory coverage floor. Within each objective, differences are meaningful enough that no persisted option can be eliminated without a user preference about cost, time, or assurance depth.
